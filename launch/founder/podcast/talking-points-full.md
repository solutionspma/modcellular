## 5-MINUTE GUEST SPOT

**Context:** Brief guest appearance on a tech/crypto podcast. Host gives you 5 minutes to explain Mod Cellular.

---

### INTRODUCTION (0:00-0:30)

**JASON:**  
"Thanks for having me. I'm Jason Harris, founder of Mod Cellular. We're building the world's first decentralized communication network — basically, a way to message people even when you have no cell service. And users earn crypto tokens for relaying traffic.

The idea came from personal frustration. I was [personal anecdote — hiking/traveling/at a concert] with zero service, and I thought: my phone has WiFi capabilities, Bluetooth, GPS — why can't it just find SOME way to send a message? That question led to 18 months of building Mod Cellular."

---

### PROBLEM (0:30-1:30)

**HOST:** "So what's the core problem you're solving?"

**JASON:**  
"Two problems, actually. First: carrier monopolies. 6.9 billion people depend on three companies — AT&T, Verizon, T-Mobile. When you're in a dead zone, you're just... offline. Cell towers are centralized points of failure.

Second: the economic model is backwards. Traditional carriers charge users monthly fees. But in a decentralized network, users provide the infrastructure — they should be earning revenue, not paying it.

So we flipped the model. Mod Cellular works without carriers, and it pays users for relaying messages."

---

### SOLUTION (1:30-3:00)

**HOST:** "How does it actually work?"

**JASON:**  
"Great question. The app scans for WiFi, Bluetooth devices, mesh nodes, and satellite signals. It scores each link based on strength, latency, and bandwidth, then picks the best route. Messages are split into 250-byte micro-packets and sent across multiple paths for redundancy.

If WiFi drops, we automatically switch to Bluetooth mesh. If that fails, we queue the message offline using DTN — Delay-Tolerant Networking — which is the same tech NASA uses for Mars rovers. Messages stay in the queue for up to 7 days and deliver when you reconnect.

And here's the economic flywheel: when your phone relays someone else's message, you earn MODX tokens. This is the DePIN model — Decentralized Physical Infrastructure Networks. Helium did this for IoT and hit a billion-dollar valuation. We're doing it for communication, which is a $1.6 trillion market."

---

### TRACTION (3:00-4:00)

**HOST:** "Where are you in terms of launch?"

**JASON:**  
"We're pre-launch right now with 25,000 waitlist signups, growing 40% week-over-week. Beta launches Q1 2026 with 1,000 users. Public launch Q2 on iOS and Android. Token launch Q3 with DEX listing.

The early community is incredibly engaged — 18% referral rate, which tells us we've hit product-market fit. People get it immediately: 'Wait, I can message in dead zones AND earn crypto?' It's a no-brainer."

---

### VISION (4:00-5:00)

**HOST:** "What's the long-term vision here?"

**JASON:**  
"Replace carriers entirely. I want a world where people don't pay phone bills — they earn from the network they build.

Short-term, we're focused on use cases where traditional carriers fail: dead zones, natural disasters, protests, international travel. But long-term, this scales to voice, video, IoT, emergency services — the entire telecom stack.

We're also in talks with hardware manufacturers about pre-installing Mod Cellular on phones. Imagine buying a phone and it just... works, globally, without a SIM card. That's the future."

**HOST:** "That's wild. How can people get involved?"

**JASON:**  
"Join the waitlist at modcellular.network. We're onboarding beta users in waves starting Q1. And if you're an investor or potential partner, reach out — jason@modcellular.network. We're raising our seed round right now."

### ADDITIONAL TOPICS TO COVER

#### Token Economics (2-3 minutes)
"MODX is an ERC-20 token with a 1 billion supply. Users earn tokens based on contribution type:
- Mesh relay: 0.05 MODX
- Micro-packet routing: 0.03 MODX
- DTN storage: 0.02 MODX
- Fallback routing: 0.01 MODX

Top relayers in urban areas earn $50-500/month. We also have a reputation system — 0-100 score based on packets relayed, uptime, and quality. Higher reputation = more earnings.

The economic model is sustainable because as the network grows, MODX demand increases, driving token value up, which attracts more users, which strengthens the network. It's a positive feedback loop."

#### Privacy & Security (2-3 minutes)
"We use end-to-end encryption inspired by the Signal Protocol. Only sender and recipient can decrypt messages. No metadata collection, no government backdoors.

Traditional carriers can see who you message, when, and where. Even encrypted apps like WhatsApp leak metadata. With Mod Cellular, because messages route peer-to-peer, there's no central server logging activity.

For protesters in authoritarian countries, this is life-or-death. For privacy advocates, it's just common sense."

#### Emergency Use Cases (2-3 minutes)
"We have an emergency SOS broadcast system. If you hit the SOS button, it broadcasts to all nearby devices at priority 999, even without internet.

During Hurricane [X], cell towers went dark for days. Mesh networks were the only communication that survived. We're building infrastructure that's resilient by design.

First responders, disaster relief teams, and military units are already reaching out about pilots."

#### Competition (2-3 minutes)
"People compare us to WhatsApp, but WhatsApp requires internet. We work in dead zones.

People compare us to Starlink, but Starlink needs a $500 satellite dish. We work on any smartphone.

People compare us to Helium, but Helium is IoT. We're communication — a market 30 times larger.

The reality is: we're not competing with anyone. We're creating a new category. Decentralized telecom didn't exist before Mod Cellular."

#### Founder Journey (2-3 minutes)
"I spent six months just researching. I read every paper on mesh networking, DTN, and blockchain incentives. I studied Helium's tokenomics for weeks.

The technical stack is complex: React Native for mobile, Bluetooth Low Energy for mesh, Supabase for backend, Solidity for smart contracts. We've built over 100 production files across five build packs.

The hardest part wasn't the tech — it was distilling a complex idea into something anyone could understand. My mom still asks, 'So... it's like WiFi?' And I say, 'Sort of, but better.'"

### HOST QUESTIONS TO PREPARE FOR

**"What happens if everyone uses Mod Cellular and no one has cell service? Doesn't the network collapse?"**  
*Answer:* "Actually, the opposite. The more users, the stronger the mesh network. Think of it like roads — more roads mean more routes. If 10,000 people in a city use Mod Cellular, you have 10,000 potential relays. Messages find a path even if half the network is offline."

**"How do you prevent malicious relayers from snooping on messages?"**  
*Answer:* "End-to-end encryption. Even relayers can't decrypt the content — they're just forwarding encrypted packets. It's like mailing a locked box through the postal service. The delivery person can't open it."

**"What's your defensibility? Can't Facebook or Google just build this?"**  
*Answer:* "First-mover advantage in DePIN. Helium proved network effects are everything. Our early users are building the mesh infrastructure — phones, relays, coverage. A competitor would have to rebuild that from scratch. Also, Big Tech has no incentive to disrupt their own carrier partnerships."

**"How do you handle regulatory issues? Governments might not like decentralized, untrackable communication."**  
*Answer:* "We're compliant with encryption laws in every jurisdiction we operate in. If a government bans E2E encryption, we geofence that region. But fundamentally, privacy is a human right. We're not going to build backdoors just because governments want surveillance."

**"What's the worst-case scenario for Mod Cellular?"**  
*Answer:* "Worst case: we don't hit critical mass in enough cities, network effects don't kick in, and we become a niche tool for hikers and preppers. But even that's a viable business — there are 50 million serious hikers in the US alone."
